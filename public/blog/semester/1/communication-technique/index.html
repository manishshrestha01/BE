Introduction

Communication Technique in Semester 1 is designed as a practical technical communication course for first-year engineering students. The syllabus focuses on how engineers communicate usable information, not just how they write grammatically correct sentences. Students are trained to think about audience, purpose, and genre before drafting content, then build documents that help readers take action. This makes the subject directly useful for lab documentation, project reporting, and professional workplace communication.

The official course description emphasizes a rhetorical approach: the same writing style does not fit every technical situation. Students are expected to choose structure, tone, and detail level according to reader needs. The syllabus therefore progresses from fundamentals and ethics to workplace messages, technical prose, document design, visuals, proposals, instructional writing, usability reporting, online communication, and oral presentation. This week-wise design helps students repeatedly apply concepts through class exercises and assignments.

If this course is studied consistently, it improves both exam answers and practical communication quality in other engineering subjects. Clear writing, structured reports, and effective presentations become an academic advantage in all later semesters. This guide follows the same week-based structure from the syllabus and keeps the content aligned with technical communication tasks expected in Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering.

What you will learn

  • Practice technical writing strategies for engineering contexts.
  • Apply core concepts to make correct decisions while writing technical documents.
  • Develop and refine a strong working draft for technical communication tasks.
  • Test documents with target users and use feedback for improvement.
  • Present usable information on specialized and technical subjects.
  • Write technical proposals, reports, and instructional documents.
  • Deliver oral technical presentations in structured format.
  • Communicate professionally in workplace scenarios.

Syllabus Overview

Weeks 1-2: Audience, Purpose, Genre, Ethics, and Research

  • Thinking about audience, purpose, and genre.
  • Leading and misleading the reader.
  • Ethics at work and ethics for students.
  • How ethics is related to technical communication.
  • Researching technical subjects.

Weeks 3-4: Workplace Messages and Technical Prose

  • Writing email and letters for the workplace.
  • Writing messages: email, memos, and letters.
  • Writing messages: informative, positive, negative, and persuasive.
  • Writing technical prose: clarity, cohesion, conciseness, and parallelism.

Weeks 5-6: Document Design and Visual Communication

  • Designing technical documents and page layout.
  • Communicating through visuals in technical contexts.

Weeks 7-8: Proposals and How-to Documents

  • Writing winning proposals.
  • Writing how-to documents: instructions, procedures, and manuals.

Weeks 9-10: Usability Testing and Reporting Technical Information (Part 1)

  • Testing and reporting document usability.
  • Reporting technical information: recommendation reports.

Weeks 11-12: Reporting Technical Information (Part 2) and Online Access

  • Reporting technical information: white papers or information reports.
  • Reporting technical information (part 3) and accessing technical documents online.
  • Sharing documents electronically.

Weeks 13-14: Writing Online Documents and Status/Progress Reports

  • Taking technical communication online: writing online documents.
  • Reporting technical information: status or progress reports.

Weeks 15-16: Oral Technical Presentation and Project Presentation

  • Oral presentation of technical reports.
  • Student presentations of major project work.

Important Topics

  • Audience, purpose, and genre mapping before drafting.
  • Ethics in technical communication and avoiding misleading readers.
  • Research strategy for technical topics.
  • Workplace writing: emails, memos, and formal letters.
  • Message classification: informative, positive, negative, persuasive.
  • Technical prose quality: clarity, cohesion, conciseness, parallelism.
  • Document/page design and visual communication principles.
  • Proposal writing and instruction/procedure/manual writing.
  • Usability testing and recommendation/information report formats.
  • Online technical communication, status reports, and oral presentation flow.

Practice Questions

  1. Explain how audience and purpose change the structure of a technical document with one engineering example.
  2. Write a short workplace memo for a technical issue update and identify ethical communication considerations.
  3. Differentiate informative, persuasive, and negative messages with format-specific examples.
  4. Design an outline for a how-to document and describe how you would test its usability.
  5. Prepare a proposal skeleton for a student engineering mini-project including objective, scope, and timeline.
  6. Compare recommendation report and information report in terms of intent, structure, and expected conclusion.
  7. Describe key considerations when converting a printed technical document into an online format.
  8. Draft a 2-minute oral presentation plan for reporting major project progress.
