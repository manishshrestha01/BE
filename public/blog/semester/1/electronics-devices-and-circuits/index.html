Introduction

Electronics Devices and Circuits introduces the device-level principles that power modern electronic systems. In Semester 1 of Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course starts from semiconductor fundamentals and gradually moves toward transistor models, amplifier analysis, feedback systems, oscillators, and operational amplifier applications. This progression is important because students first learn why a device works physically, then how to bias and model it, and finally how to use it in practical circuits.

The early units on PN junction, Zener diode, BJT, and rectifier/regulator circuits provide the core base for understanding analog electronics. Students learn characteristics, biasing, load-line concepts, switching behavior, and stabilization factors. These ideas are used repeatedly in later instrumentation, communication, and embedded electronics topics. Mid-course units on JFET/MOSFET and small-signal BJT models (re-model) train students to derive gain, impedance, and circuit behavior mathematically, which is critical for exam problems and design-oriented practical work.

The final units cover multistage amplifiers, large-signal amplifiers, feedback/oscillation concepts, and op-amp blocks such as inverting, non-inverting, summing, integrator, and differentiator circuits. If students combine diagram practice, characteristic interpretation, and stepwise circuit analysis, this subject becomes manageable and highly useful. This guide organizes those requirements in a structured form aligned with your official syllabus and practical expectations.

What you will learn

  • Explain semiconductor behavior, PN junction formation, and diode operation modes.
  • Analyze Zener diode behavior and basic regulator use cases.
  • Interpret BJT construction, configurations, characteristics, and biasing methods.
  • Evaluate rectifier, filter, clipping, clamping, and regulated power supply circuits.
  • Work with JFET/MOSFET characteristics and FET bias/load-line concepts.
  • Apply small-signal transistor models to compute gain and impedance.
  • Compare Class A/B/AB amplifiers, push-pull design, and crossover distortion.
  • Use feedback and op-amp fundamentals to design basic analog functional blocks.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Semiconductor Diode (5 hrs)

  • Insulator, semiconductor, and conductor basics.
  • Carrier physics, donor/acceptor impurities, n-type and p-type materials.
  • PN junction formation, barrier potential, forward/reverse bias operation.
  • Breakdown, junction/diffusion capacitance, and switching times.
  • Zener diode characteristics and applications.

Unit II: Bi-polar Junction Transistor (6 hrs)

  • BJT introduction, construction, modes, and configurations.
  • Current gains and relationship among CB/CE/CC forms.
  • Input/output characteristics and biasing methods.
  • DC/AC load line, Q-point, amplifier/switch behavior, stabilization factor.

Unit III: DC Power Supply (5 hrs)

  • Rectifier types, ripple factor, efficiency, and waveform measures.
  • Filtering process and shunt capacitor behavior.
  • Clipper and clamper circuits.
  • Regulated vs unregulated supply and transistor regulator variants.

Unit IV: The Field Effect Transistor (FET) (4 hrs)

  • FET vs BJT comparison.
  • JFET construction, operation, drain/transfer characteristics, and parameters.
  • FET biasing and DC load line.
  • DMOSFET and EMOSFET working principles.

Unit V: The Small Signal Low Frequency Analysis Model of BJT (6 hrs)

  • AC analysis concept of BJT amplifiers.
  • re-model for different amplifier configurations.
  • Gain and impedance expressions using re-model.
  • Emitter follower analysis.

Unit VI: Multistage Amplifiers (4 hrs)

  • Need for multistage amplification and coupling methods.
  • Gain calculation in cascaded stages.
  • Two-stage RC-coupled amplifier gain/impedance expressions.
  • Darlington pair and effective beta.

Unit VII: Large Signal Amplifiers (4 hrs)

  • Large signal amplifier importance and class-based analysis.
  • Class A, B, AB amplifier behavior and efficiency trade-offs.
  • Push-pull and transformer-coupled push-pull amplifiers.
  • Cross-over distortion, efficiency, and power dissipation.

Unit VIII: Feedback Amplifiers (7 hrs)

  • Feedback amplifier classification.
  • Negative feedback advantages: gain stability, bandwidth, noise, impedances.
  • Positive feedback and Barkhausen criterion.
  • RC/LC oscillators and astable/monostable multivibrators using op-amp context.

Unit IX: Operational Amplifier (4 hrs)

  • Basic op-amp model and ideal/non-ideal properties.
  • Virtual ground concept and CMRR.
  • Inverting and non-inverting amplifier.
  • Summing, integrator, and differentiator applications.

Important Topics

  • PN junction biasing behavior and diode characteristic interpretation.
  • Zener breakdown region and regulator design logic.
  • BJT biasing methods and Q-point stabilization.
  • Rectifier-filter-regulator chain and ripple reduction.
  • JFET drain/transfer curve and parameter extraction basics.
  • Small-signal re-model gain and impedance derivations.
  • Two-stage RC-coupled and Darlington amplifier analysis.
  • Class A/B/AB amplifier efficiency and crossover distortion.
  • Negative feedback effect on gain, bandwidth, and noise.
  • Core op-amp circuits: inverting, non-inverting, summing, integrator, differentiator.

Practice Questions

  1. Explain PN junction operation under forward and reverse bias with labeled characteristic sketch.
  2. Design a simple Zener regulator and compute output regulation under load variation.
  3. For a CE transistor circuit, determine DC load line, Q-point, and expected operating region.
  4. Compare half-wave and full-wave rectifier performance in terms of ripple and efficiency.
  5. Using given JFET data, plot/interpret transfer characteristics and identify operating point.
  6. Compute voltage gain and input impedance for a transistor amplifier using re-model assumptions.
  7. Analyze a Class B push-pull amplifier and discuss crossover distortion mitigation.
  8. Design an op-amp based summing or integrator circuit and explain one practical application.
