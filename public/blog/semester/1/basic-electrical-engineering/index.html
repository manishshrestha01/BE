Introduction

Basic Electrical Engineering gives first-semester students the technical foundation needed to understand how electrical energy is generated, transmitted, converted, and used in engineering systems. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this subject is not treated as a purely theoretical branch course. Instead, it combines electrical circuit fundamentals with practical machine concepts so students can interpret real components, measurements, and operating behavior. The syllabus starts with core ideas of energy sources and modern electricity usage, then gradually builds into quantitative DC and AC circuit analysis. This progression is important because it trains students to move from conceptual understanding to formal calculations using circuit laws, network theorems, and power relations.

The DC portion develops strong analysis skills through Kirchhoff's laws, nodal and mesh methods, source conversion, star-delta transformation, and major network theorems such as Superposition, Thevenin, Norton, and Maximum Power Transfer. These tools are essential in later electronics and hardware subjects because they create a systematic way to solve unknown currents and voltages in complex networks. The AC units then extend this to sinusoidal behavior, RMS and average values, impedance-admittance relationships, resonance, power factor, and three-phase systems. Learning these topics in Semester 1 helps students build confidence in interpreting frequency-domain behavior instead of seeing electrical quantities only in static form.

The final unit on electrical machines connects circuit-level thinking to real devices: transformers, DC generators, DC motors, and induction motors. This bridge from equations to machine behavior is the key value of the course. If students maintain a routine of solving circuit problems, drawing phasor relationships, and connecting test results to theory, Basic Electrical Engineering becomes a high-scoring and highly practical subject. This article is structured to help with that workflow by covering what to learn, unit-wise content, exam-relevant topics, and practice questions aligned with the official syllabus.

What you will learn

  • Explain the role of electrical energy systems in modern engineering applications.
  • Apply voltage-divider, current-divider, KCL, and KVL in DC circuit analysis.
  • Solve DC networks using nodal analysis, mesh analysis, and source transformation.
  • Use Superposition, Thevenin, Norton, and Maximum Power Transfer theorems correctly.
  • Analyze single-phase AC circuits using phasors, impedance, power, and power factor.
  • Interpret resonance, bandwidth, and Q-factor in RLC circuits.
  • Compute line and phase values in three-phase star and delta systems.
  • Describe operation and performance basics of transformers, DC machines, and induction motors.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction (2 hrs)

  • Role of electricity in modern society.
  • Energy sources and electrical energy production.
  • Generation, transmission, and distribution of electrical energy.
  • Patterns of electricity consumption.

Unit II: DC Circuit Analysis (15 hrs)

  • Circuit concepts: lumped/distributed, linear/nonlinear, passive/active.
  • Circuit elements and properties: R, L, C in practical and geometrical context.
  • Series-parallel combinations, color coding, and star-delta transformation.
  • Power and energy calculations, open/short-circuit concepts.
  • Ideal/non-ideal sources, source conversion, divider rules, KCL/KVL.
  • Nodal and mesh analysis (without dependent sources).
  • Network theorems: Superposition, Thevenin, Norton, Maximum Power Transfer.

Unit III: Single Phase AC Circuit Analysis (10 hrs)

  • Generation of alternating quantities and electromagnetic induction basis.
  • Sinusoidal terminology, average value, and RMS value.
  • Phase algebra, impedance/admittance concepts, and power triangle.
  • Steady-state analysis of RL, RC, and RLC circuits.
  • Resonance in series/parallel RLC, bandwidth, and Q-factor impact.

Unit IV: Poly-phase AC Circuit Analysis (6 hrs)

  • Balanced three-phase supply generation and characteristics.
  • Differences between single-phase and three-phase systems.
  • Star/delta supply-load relations and line-phase conversions.
  • Three-phase power and measurement using one/two wattmeter methods.

Unit V: Electrical Machines (12 hrs)

  • Electric vs magnetic circuit similarities and differences.
  • Single-phase transformer operation, equivalent circuit, efficiency, and regulation.
  • Open-circuit and short-circuit tests of transformers.
  • DC generator construction, principles, and characteristics.
  • DC motor operation, speed control, and selection basics.
  • Single-phase and three-phase induction motor principles and uses.

Important Topics

  • KCL/KVL application strategy for multi-loop DC circuits.
  • Mesh vs nodal analysis selection and equation setup.
  • Thevenin-Norton conversion and maximum power transfer condition.
  • Phasor relationships and impedance triangle interpretation.
  • Real, reactive, and apparent power with power factor correction logic.
  • Resonance condition, bandwidth, and quality factor.
  • Line-phase relationships in three-phase star and delta connections.
  • Two-wattmeter method interpretation for three-phase power.
  • Transformer OC/SC test purpose and parameter extraction basics.
  • DC machine and induction motor operating characteristics.

Practice Questions

  1. Using KCL and KVL, solve a two-loop DC circuit and verify power balance.
  2. Reduce a given network to Thevenin equivalent and find load current for multiple RL values.
  3. Apply Superposition theorem to determine branch current in a circuit with two independent sources.
  4. For a series RLC AC circuit, compute impedance, current, phase angle, and power factor.
  5. Explain resonance in RLC circuits and derive the relation between bandwidth and Q-factor.
  6. Convert a three-phase delta load to equivalent star and compute line current.
  7. Using transformer OC and SC test data, estimate efficiency and voltage regulation.
  8. Compare operating principles and practical applications of DC motor and three-phase induction motor.
