Introduction

Engineering Economics in Semester 7 provides a structured framework for making financially sound technical decisions. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course connects economics concepts with engineering project realities such as investment selection, cost behavior, risk analysis, and replacement planning. It helps students move beyond technical feasibility toward financially justified project choices.

The course begins with basics of economics, cost concepts, and time value of money. These topics are foundational because most engineering decisions involve cash-flow timing, interest effects, and comparative alternatives. Students then learn practical evaluation methods including payback period, present/future/annual worth, IRR, ERR, and benefit-cost ratios.

The final part covers replacement strategies, risk techniques, depreciation, taxes, capital budgeting, and inflation adjustment. Together, these topics form a complete decision toolkit used in industry, consulting, and project management roles. Regular numerical practice and method comparison are essential for strong performance in this subject.

What you will learn

  • Explain core economics and engineering-economics principles used in project decisions.
  • Classify cost elements and cost types for technical and business analysis.
  • Apply time-value-of-money formulas to multiple cash-flow patterns.
  • Compute MARR, payback, equivalent worth, IRR/ERR, and BCR for project appraisal.
  • Compare alternatives with same and different useful lives.
  • Evaluate replacement decisions using defender-challenger and service-life concepts.
  • Apply risk-analysis methods including breakeven, sensitivity, decision-tree, and scenario analysis.
  • Incorporate depreciation, tax, and inflation effects in after-tax cash-flow analysis.

Syllabus Overview

Unit 1: Basics of Engineering Economics (3 hrs)

  • Definition and principles of economics.
  • Demand, supply, production, and utility introduction.
  • Definition, principles, and applications of engineering economics.
  • Common terminologies in engineering economic analysis.

Unit 2: Cost Concept and Analysis (3 hrs)

  • Elements of cost: material, labor, and expenses.
  • Cost types: direct, indirect, fixed, variable, marginal, opportunity, sunk, manufacturing, and non-manufacturing costs.

Unit 3: Interest and Time Value of Money (6 hrs)

  • Simple, compound, nominal, and effective interest rates.
  • Economic equivalence concepts and principles.
  • Equivalence formulas for single cashflow, equal-payment series, linear/geometric gradients, and mixed cashflow series.

Unit 4: Basic Methodologies of Engineering Economic Studies (6 hrs)

  • Minimum attractive rate of return (MARR) and calculation concept.
  • Payback period: simple and discounted.
  • Equivalent worth methods: present, future, and annual worth.
  • Rate of return methods: IRR and ERR.
  • Benefit-cost ratio: normal, conventional, and modified.
  • Basic concept of economic vs financial analysis.

Unit 5: Comparative Analysis of Alternatives (6 hrs)

  • Mutually exclusive, independent, and contingent projects.
  • Comparison of alternatives with same useful life.
  • Incremental analysis using IRR, ERR, and BCR.
  • Comparison of alternatives with different useful lives.
  • Repeatability, co-terminated, and capitalized-worth assumptions.
  • Selection of best project combinations.

Unit 6: Replacement Analysis (4 hrs)

  • Defender and challenger concepts.
  • Economic service life.
  • Replacement strategies for finite and infinite planning horizons.

Unit 7: Risk Analysis (6 hrs)

  • Concept and sources of risk in engineering projects.
  • Breakeven analysis.
  • Sensitivity analysis.
  • Decision-tree analysis.
  • Scenario analysis.
  • Monte Carlo simulation concept.

Unit 8: Depreciation and Taxes (6 hrs)

  • Depreciation and depletion causes and applications.
  • Depreciation methods: straight line, units of production, sum-of-years-digits, declining balance, MACRS, and sinking fund.
  • Cost and percentage depletion.
  • Direct/indirect taxes, tax rates, and VAT.
  • After-tax cashflow, NPV, and IRR calculations.

Unit 9: Capital Budgeting Decision (3 hrs)

  • Capital budgeting definition and importance.
  • Financing methods: equity, debt, and capital-structure perspectives.
  • Cost of equity, cost of debt, and overall cost of capital.

Unit 10: Inflation (2 hrs)

  • Meaning and measures of inflation.
  • Actual dollar vs constant dollar analysis.
  • Market and inflation-free interest rates.
  • Relationship between inflation and adjusted interest rates.

Important Topics

  • Cashflow equivalence and interest-rate conversion.
  • MARR and project screening logic.
  • IRR/ERR interpretation under multiple alternatives.
  • Incremental analysis for mutually exclusive projects.
  • Defender-challenger replacement decisions.
  • Sensitivity and decision-tree based risk analysis.
  • Depreciation method comparison and tax impact.
  • After-tax NPV and IRR formulation.
  • Cost of capital in budgeting decisions.
  • Inflation-adjusted discounting and constant-dollar analysis.

Practice Questions

  1. Classify given project costs into direct, indirect, fixed, variable, and opportunity categories.
  2. Solve a cashflow-equivalence problem involving nominal and effective interest rates.
  3. Evaluate a project using simple/discounted payback, NPV, and annual worth.
  4. Perform incremental IRR or BCR analysis for two mutually exclusive alternatives.
  5. Find the economic service life of a machine and recommend replacement timing.
  6. Apply sensitivity and breakeven analysis to a project with uncertain demand.
  7. Compute depreciation schedule and after-tax cashflow using one selected depreciation method.
  8. Compare project feasibility under actual-dollar and constant-dollar assumptions with inflation effects.
