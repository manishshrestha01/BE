Introduction

Entrepreneurship and Professional Practice in Semester 7 prepares students to move from pure technical learning toward real market and professional environments. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course combines startup thinking, business planning, ethics, and legal awareness so students can evaluate both entrepreneurial and professional career paths in ICT. It is designed to make students practical, responsible, and opportunity oriented.

The first part of the course focuses on entrepreneurship fundamentals, innovation, idea generation, and startup feasibility analysis. Students study ecosystem dynamics, market and competitor analysis, team formation, strategy, and basic financial planning. This structure helps convert rough ideas into viable value propositions with measurable business assumptions.

The later units on business canvas, pitching, professional ethics, and legal frameworks make the course highly relevant for both founders and employed engineers. By learning intellectual property, ICT laws, compliance requirements, and professional codes of conduct, students build decision-making maturity that is essential in product development, consulting, and leadership roles.

What you will learn

  • Explain entrepreneurship fundamentals, types of entrepreneurs, and enterprise lifecycle.
  • Apply creativity and innovation methods to generate and validate startup ideas.
  • Analyze startup ecosystem, competition, market size, and go-to-market strategy.
  • Define startup vision, mission, SMART goals, and team-building approach.
  • Prepare business model, value proposition, and lean canvas artifacts.
  • Develop business plans and stakeholder-oriented pitch decks.
  • Understand professional ethics, conflict of interest, and social responsibility in engineering practice.
  • Interpret key legal issues including intellectual property, ICT laws, business registration, and taxation.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction to Entrepreneurship (4 hrs)

  • Definition of entrepreneurship and core terminology.
  • Startup, enterprise, entrepreneur, entrepreneurial attitude, intention, and behavior.
  • Entrepreneurship vs employment comparison.
  • Types of entrepreneurs and their characteristics.
  • Enterprise lifecycle, role, importance, and challenges.
  • Emerging technologies and trends in global and local startups.

Unit II: Creativity, Innovation and MVP (3 hrs)

  • Creativity, innovation, and opportunity-recognition concepts.
  • Sources of new business ideas and idea-generation tools.
  • Types of innovation: product, process, and business model.
  • Value proposition and minimum viable product (MVP).

Unit III: Startup Foundational Analysis (8 hrs)

  • Environmental analysis: ecosystem, supports, and barriers.
  • Competitor analysis and sources of competitive advantage.
  • Market research and target-customer profiling.
  • Key terms: SAM, TAM, SOM, and go-to-market strategy.
  • Branding and promotional strategies.
  • Business strategy development: vision, mission, and SMART goals.
  • Team-building for startup founders and early employees.
  • Financial analysis: revenue streams, cost structure, projections, and startup financing (seed, angel, venture capital).

Unit IV: Business Modeling, Planning and Pitching (6 hrs)

  • Business canvas types: business model, value proposition, and lean canvas.
  • Importance and structure of business plans.
  • Business pitch contents for different stakeholders.
  • Pitch-delivery skills and presentation quality.

Unit V: Ethics and Responsibilities for Professional Practice (4 hrs)

  • Professional roles and responsibilities.
  • Conflict of interest and whistleblowing.
  • Corporate social responsibility.
  • Code of conduct references: Nepal Engineering Council, IEEE, and ACM.

Unit VI: Legal Issues for Professional Practice (5 hrs)

  • Intellectual property basics: copyright, patent, design, trademark, and trade secrets.
  • IP-related legal framework.
  • ICT policy, Electronic Transaction Act, and Communication Act in Nepal.
  • Startup legal processes: business registration, taxation, and compliance.

Important Topics

  • Entrepreneurial attitude and opportunity mapping.
  • MVP design and value proposition fit.
  • SAM, TAM, SOM estimation logic.
  • Go-to-market strategy choices.
  • Revenue-cost structure modeling for startups.
  • Canvas and business-plan alignment.
  • Pitch structure for investors and partners.
  • Professional ethics in engineering decisions.
  • Intellectual property and compliance basics.
  • Nepal ICT legal and registration workflow.

Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate entrepreneur, startup, and enterprise with one local example.
  2. Develop an MVP concept and value proposition for a technology problem in your area.
  3. Prepare a startup ecosystem and competitor map for your proposed idea.
  4. Estimate TAM, SAM, and SOM for a selected product and justify assumptions.
  5. Create a one-page business canvas and explain each block briefly.
  6. Prepare a short pitch deck outline for an angel investor.
  7. Discuss one conflict-of-interest scenario in professional software practice and propose ethical handling.
  8. List legal steps to register a startup in Nepal and identify two key compliance requirements.
