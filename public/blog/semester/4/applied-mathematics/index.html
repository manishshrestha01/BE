Introduction

Applied Mathematics in Semester 4 extends earlier calculus and algebra into advanced tools used in engineering analysis. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course is structured around complex-variable methods, transform-domain techniques, and partial differential equation models. The goal is not only to solve equations mechanically but to develop mathematical modeling ability for dynamic systems, signal behavior, and field-type problems.

The first major unit on Complex Analysis introduces analytic functions, Cauchy-Riemann conditions, line integrals in the complex plane, and core theorems such as Cauchy's integral theorem and formula. Taylor and Laurent expansions with residues prepare students for contour-style reasoning and advanced integration viewpoints. The Z-transform unit then shifts to discrete-time systems where transform properties, shifting rules, and inverse methods are used to solve difference equations, which is highly relevant for digital computation and signal processing contexts.

The final part of the syllabus covers PDE and Fourier methods. Separation of variables with one-dimensional wave and heat equations builds strong physical interpretation skills, while two-dimensional heat and Laplace equations connect mathematics with boundary-value modeling. Fourier integrals and transforms complete the toolkit by enabling frequency-domain representation and convolution-based analysis. With systematic practice on derivations and problem setup, this course becomes a strong analytical support subject for later engineering courses.

What you will learn

  • Work with complex functions and test analyticity using Cauchy-Riemann conditions.
  • Evaluate line integrals in complex plane with Cauchy integral results.
  • Use Taylor and Laurent series with residue concepts for complex-function analysis.
  • Apply Z-transform properties and inverse techniques for sequence-domain problems.
  • Solve linear difference equations using Z-transform methods.
  • Solve PDEs using separation of variables for heat and wave equations.
  • Interpret Laplace equation solutions on rectangular and circular boundaries.
  • Use Fourier integrals and Fourier transforms, including convolution and Parseval identity.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Complex Analysis (17 hrs.)

  • Complex numbers, geometric representation, polar form, powers, and roots.
  • Sets and functions in complex plane; limits, continuity, and derivatives (conceptual view).
  • Analytic functions and Cauchy-Riemann equations as necessary and sufficient conditions.
  • Polar form of Cauchy-Riemann equations and Laplace equation.
  • Harmonic functions and harmonic conjugates.
  • Line integrals in complex plane and basic evaluations.
  • Cauchy integral theorem, Cauchy integral formula, and higher-order formula (without proof).
  • Taylor and Laurent series expansions (without proof) and related problems.
  • Singularities, zeros, residues, and Cauchy residue theorem (without proof).
  • Conformal mapping with linear fractional (bilinear) transformation.

Unit II: Z-Transform and its Applications (10 hrs.)

  • Z-transform of elementary functions and core properties.
  • Shifting theorems with initial and final value theorems.
  • Inverse Z-transform using division, expansion, partial fraction, and residue methods.
  • Application to difference equations and discrete-time solution setup.

Unit III: Partial Differential Equations (12 hrs.)

  • PDE fundamentals and solution by variable-separation method.
  • One-dimensional wave equation and solution techniques.
  • One-dimensional heat equation and related solution patterns.
  • Two-dimensional heat equation and Laplace equation for rectangular boundaries.
  • Laplace equation in polar form and solution for circular boundaries.

Unit IV: Fourier Integral and Transform (6 hrs.)

  • Fourier integral, Fourier sine integral, and Fourier cosine integral.
  • Fourier integral in complex form.
  • Fourier transform and inverse transform.
  • Fourier sine/cosine transforms and inverse forms.
  • Convolution theorem and Parseval identity with problem applications.

Important Topics

  • Analyticity tests via Cartesian and polar Cauchy-Riemann forms.
  • Harmonic and conjugate harmonic function construction.
  • Cauchy theorem and integral-formula application patterns.
  • Laurent expansion, residue extraction, and singularity classification.
  • Z-transform shifting rules and inverse-method selection.
  • Difference-equation solution using transform-domain techniques.
  • Separation of variables for heat and wave PDEs.
  • Boundary-condition handling in rectangular and circular domains.
  • Fourier integral and transform pair derivations.
  • Convolution theorem and Parseval identity use in evaluations.

Practice Questions

  1. Check analyticity of a complex function and verify Cauchy-Riemann conditions.
  2. Evaluate a contour integral using Cauchy integral theorem or formula.
  3. Expand a function into Taylor or Laurent series and classify its singularity.
  4. Compute residues and evaluate an integral using residue theorem approach.
  5. Find Z-transform and inverse Z-transform of given sequences using two different methods.
  6. Solve a linear difference equation using Z-transform.
  7. Solve one-dimensional heat or wave equation by separation of variables.
  8. Compute Fourier transform of a function and verify one result using convolution or Parseval relation.
