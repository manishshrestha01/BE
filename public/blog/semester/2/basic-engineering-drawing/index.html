Introduction

Basic Engineering Drawing in Semester 2 is a practical subject that builds core visualization and representation skills required in engineering. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course is not limited to neat sketching; it trains students to read and produce technically correct drawings using standard methods, notations, and conventions. Students start from manual drafting fundamentals such as instruments, drawing sheet setup, lettering style, and dimensioning rules, then gradually move into geometry construction and projection-based interpretation. This progression helps learners convert abstract shapes into accurate views and measurable engineering drawings.

A major part of the syllabus focuses on orthographic multiview drawing and sectional views. These topics are essential because engineers must communicate internal and external features of objects without ambiguity. The course also includes development of surfaces for solids, which is useful in fabrication-oriented applications. Projection methods such as isometric, oblique, and perspective are introduced to improve three-dimensional understanding and presentation quality. By practicing these methods regularly, students improve both visual reasoning and drafting precision.

The final unit adds computer-aided drawing with AutoCAD and basic 3D exposure. This is a practical bridge between manual drawing discipline and modern digital drafting workflows. Students who follow a routine of stepwise construction, correct dimensioning, and frequent sheet practice generally perform well in both continuous assessment and semester-end practical evaluation.

What you will learn

  • Use engineering drawing instruments, sheets, and layout standards correctly.
  • Apply technical lettering and dimensioning rules with proper notations.
  • Construct geometric entities including polygons, tangents, and engineering curves.
  • Create orthographic multiview drawings by selecting correct views.
  • Draw full, half, and offset sectional views with standard hatching.
  • Develop surfaces of truncated and frustum forms of common solids.
  • Visualize objects using isometric, oblique, and one-point perspective methods.
  • Use basic AutoCAD 2D commands and introductory 3D drawing operations.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction to Engineering Drawing (3 hrs)

  • Manual drawing instruments, drafting machines, drawing paper/materials, and drawing-sheet layout planning.
  • Preparation for drawing and cautions in instrument use.
  • Technical lettering and dimensioning: single-stroke letters, vertical/slant lettering, and numerals.
  • Dimensioning terms and notations, theory/system of dimensioning, scales, units, and general dimensioning rules.

Unit II: Geometrical Constructions (7 hrs)

  • Construction with lines and angles, including bisection, trisection, and proportional division.
  • Construction of polygons, tangents, circles, arcs, and open/cross belt tangents.
  • Conic and engineering curves: ellipse (four-center), parabola (tangent), hyperbola (eccentricity), cycloid, involute, Archimedean spiral, and helix.

Unit III: Multi-view Drawings and Sectional Views (15 hrs)

  • Introduction to projection of points, lines, and planes with orthographic projection principles.
  • Selection of views and procedure for creating multiview drawings.
  • Sectional views: full section, half section, offset section, and hatching conventions.

Unit IV: Developments of Surfaces (4 hrs)

  • Introduction to surface development for engineering solids.
  • Complete development of truncated right solids: cylinder, cone, pyramid, and prism.
  • Development of frustums of right cone and right pyramid.

Unit V: Isometric, Oblique and Perspective Projections (6 hrs)

  • Introduction to axonometric projection and isometric drawing methods/procedure.
  • Introduction to oblique projection and oblique drawing.
  • Perspective projection fundamentals, object position, and one-point perspective construction.

Unit VI: Symbols and Computer Aided Drawing (10 hrs)

  • Electrical and electronics symbols used in technical drawings.
  • AutoCAD introduction with basic 2D commands: Line, Circle, Polyline, Rectangle, Hatch, Fillet, Chamfer, Trim, Extend, Offset, and Dimstyle.
  • Basics of 3D drawings in laboratory demonstration and practice mode.

Important Topics

  • Drawing-sheet layout and instrument handling discipline.
  • Lettering standards and aligned/unaligned dimensioning conventions.
  • Construction of conics and engineering curves using prescribed methods.
  • Orthographic projection logic and view-selection strategy.
  • Sectional view types and correct hatching practices.
  • Development of truncated solids and frustum surfaces.
  • Difference between isometric and oblique representation.
  • One-point perspective construction steps and vanishing-point handling.
  • Electrical/electronics symbol interpretation in drafting tasks.
  • AutoCAD command workflow for fast and accurate 2D drawing.

Practice Questions

  1. Prepare a standard drawing sheet layout and demonstrate correct lettering and dimensioning for a given object.
  2. Construct an ellipse by four-center method and a parabola by tangent method on the same sheet.
  3. Draw multiview orthographic projections of a given solid and justify the chosen principal views.
  4. Create full and half sectional views of a component and apply hatching conventions properly.
  5. Develop the complete surface of a truncated cone or pyramid with all construction lines.
  6. Draw one object in isometric and oblique projections and compare clarity of representation.
  7. Construct a one-point perspective view of a simple block arrangement with proper proportion.
  8. Replicate a manual drawing in AutoCAD using core 2D commands and apply dimensions and hatch.
