Introduction

Applied Chemistry in Semester 2 is designed to give engineering students a practical chemical foundation rather than purely theoretical chemistry review. The syllabus covers electrochemistry, battery technology, corrosion, environmental chemistry, transition elements, organic reaction types, engineering materials, and polymers. These areas are selected because they directly connect to real systems used in electronics, manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure.

The first unit on electrochemistry is particularly important for computer engineering students because it supports understanding of batteries, electrochemical behavior, and corrosion control. Environmental chemistry then introduces pollution mechanisms and water quality parameters, which are essential for sustainable engineering decisions. The subject also includes transition metal chemistry and organic reaction basics to strengthen material and process awareness.

The final units on engineering materials and polymers connect chemistry to industry-focused topics such as cement, paints, explosives, sensors, photovoltaic cells, and polymer classes. If studied with application examples and short concept notes, Applied Chemistry becomes a strong support subject for multidisciplinary engineering understanding and exam performance.

What you will learn

  • Explain electrolytic and galvanic cell operation with electrode concepts.
  • Apply electrochemical series and corrosion principles in material selection.
  • Compare battery types and describe key working mechanisms.
  • Analyze air, water, and soil pollution causes, effects, and controls.
  • Interpret core water quality indicators used in engineering practice.
  • Describe transition element properties and engineering applications.
  • Classify basic organic reaction types such as substitution and elimination.
  • Understand engineering materials and polymer categories with practical uses.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Electrochemistry and Battery Technology (6 hrs)

  • Electrolytic cell and applications.
  • Galvanic cell examples, electrode potential, EMF, and cell representation.
  • Electrochemical series and engineering relevance.
  • Electrochemical theory of corrosion and prevention methods.
  • Battery importance and classification: primary, secondary, and reserve.
  • Construction, working, and applications of Zn-Cu, Ni-Cd, Lithium-ion, and Sodium-ion batteries.

Unit II: Environmental Chemistry (5 hrs)

  • Air, water, and soil pollution: causes, effects, and control methods.
  • Water quality analysis: alkalinity, hardness, free chlorine, dissolved oxygen, and chemical oxygen demand.

Unit III: Transition Elements and its Applications (4 hrs)

  • Introduction to transition elements and their position in periodic table.
  • Characteristics: electronic configuration, atomic radii, and variable oxidation states.
  • Complex formation, color, magnetic behavior, and catalytic property.
  • Applications of transition metals in engineering.

Unit IV: Types of Organic Reactions (4 hrs)

  • Substitution reactions SN1 and SN2: mechanism, kinetics, stereochemistry, reactivity, and influencing factors.
  • Elimination reactions E1 and E2: mechanism, kinetics, Saytzeff orientation rule, reactivity, and influencing factors.

Unit V: Engineering Materials and its Applications (7 hrs)

  • Explosives: introduction, preparation, properties, and applications of TNT and TNG.
  • Cement: types, manufacture of Portland cement, and setting/hardening mechanism.
  • Paints: introduction, key properties, and constituents.
  • Sensors: basic principle and applications.
  • Photovoltaic cells: basic principle and applications.

Unit VI: Polymers and its Applications (4 hrs)

  • Polymer basics with addition, condensation, and copolymerization.
  • Preparation, properties, and uses of PVC, Teflon, silicone rubber, and neoprene.
  • Conducting and non-conducting polymers with examples.
  • Biodegradable and non-biodegradable polymer concepts and uses.

Important Topics

  • Electrolytic vs galvanic cell comparison and electrode roles.
  • EMF calculation and cell representation format.
  • Electrochemical series based reactivity judgment.
  • Corrosion mechanism and prevention strategy mapping.
  • Battery classification and chemistry of Zn-Cu, Ni-Cd, Li-ion, and Na-ion cells.
  • Water quality parameters: alkalinity, hardness, free chlorine, DO, and COD.
  • Transition element property trends and applications.
  • SN1/SN2 and E1/E2 reaction pathways and kinetics.
  • Engineering material chemistry behind cement and paints.
  • Sensor and photovoltaic material principles.
  • Polymerization routes and polymer property-use relationships (PVC, Teflon, silicone, neoprene).

Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate electrolytic and galvanic cells with neat examples and electrode reactions.
  2. Explain electrochemical corrosion mechanism and propose practical prevention methods for engineering components.
  3. Compare Zn-Cu, Ni-Cd, Lithium-ion, and Sodium-ion batteries in terms of construction, working, and use.
  4. Write short notes on alkalinity, hardness, free chlorine, dissolved oxygen, and COD in water analysis.
  5. Describe key characteristics of transition elements and their catalytic significance.
  6. Differentiate SN1/SN2 and E1/E2 reactions with mechanisms and major factors.
  7. Explain properties and engineering uses of TNT/TNG, Portland cement, and paints.
  8. Discuss polymerization types and compare conducting/non-conducting and biodegradable/non-biodegradable polymers.
