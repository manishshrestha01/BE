Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering semester 2 tutorial for Applied Physics (course code PHY 110) with syllabus, key topics, concepts, and 5 practice questions.

Introduction

Applied Physics in Semester 2 connects core physical principles with practical engineering interpretation. The syllabus spans oscillations, wave motion, acoustics, photonics, capacitors and dielectrics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and thermodynamics with heat transfer. For BE Computer Engineering students, these topics build scientific intuition that later supports electronics, communication systems, instrumentation, and computational modeling.

The course starts with mechanical and wave behavior to establish motion and energy concepts, then moves into acoustics and photonics where real-world applications like ultrasonics, laser systems, and optical fibers are emphasized. The electrical physics units on dielectrics and electromagnetism strengthen understanding of capacitive behavior, field interactions, and EM wave propagation.

The final units introduce modern physics and thermal science through quantum mechanics and thermodynamics. Concepts such as de Broglie waves, wave functions, Schrodinger framework, laws of thermodynamics, and heat transfer mechanisms are essential for broad engineering literacy. This guide organizes these units for stepwise preparation and exam-focused revision.

What you will learn

  • Analyze free, damped, and forced oscillation behavior.
  • Apply wave motion relations to speed, intensity, and standing wave problems.
  • Explain acoustic principles, reverberation, and ultrasonic applications.
  • Describe laser and optical fiber principles with engineering use cases.
  • Evaluate capacitor and dielectric behavior in practical circuits.
  • Interpret electromagnetic oscillation and EM wave equations conceptually.
  • Apply key quantum mechanics ideas including wave-particle duality.
  • Use thermodynamics and heat transfer laws for engineering problem setup.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Mechanical Oscillation (6 hrs)

  • Free, damped, and forced oscillation with physical meaning and equations.
  • Compound pendulum, minimum/maximum time period, and interchangeability of suspension and oscillation points.
  • Torsion pendulum and determination of modulus of rigidity.

Unit II: Wave Motion (4 hrs)

  • Introduction to wave motion, wave velocity, and particle velocity.
  • Types of waves and engineering applications.
  • Speed of wave on stretched string and energy/power/intensity of progressive wave.
  • Standing waves, resonance, and sonometer-based problems.

Unit III: Acoustics (4 hrs.)

  • Classification of sound waves.
  • Acoustics of building, reverberation, and absorption coefficient.
  • Noise pollution, control techniques, and Sabine equation.
  • Introduction, production, and applications of ultrasonic waves.
  • Ultrasonic methods in non-destructive testing.

Unit IV: Photonics (6 hrs.)

  • Laser fundamentals: induced absorption, spontaneous emission, stimulated emission, population inversion, pumping, and metastable state.
  • He-Ne laser, semiconductor laser, and major engineering applications.
  • Fiber optics introduction and types of optical fiber.
  • Principle of light propagation in optical fiber (acceptance angle and numerical aperture).
  • Applications in communication and optical fiber sensors.

Unit V: Capacitor and Dielectric (6 hrs.)

  • Capacitor fundamentals, types, and charging/discharging behavior.
  • Dielectric constant, electric flux density, and polarization.
  • Polarization in free space and Gauss law in dielectric medium.
  • Electronic and ionic polarization with Clausius-Mossotti relation.

Unit VI: Electromagnetism (6 hrs.)

  • Electromagnetic oscillations: LC, damped LCR, forced oscillation, resonance, and quality factor.
  • Maxwell equations in integral and differential forms.
  • Continuity equation and relation between electric field, magnetic field, and speed of light.
  • Wave equations in free space and dielectric medium; verification of light as EM wave.

Unit VII: Quantum Mechanics (5 hrs.)

  • Inadequacy of classical mechanics and importance of quantum mechanics.
  • Matter waves and de Broglie equation.
  • Wave function and its significance.
  • Energy and momentum operators.
  • Time-independent and time-dependent Schrodinger wave equations with introductory applications.

Unit VIII: Fundamentals of Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer (8 hrs.)

  • Applications of thermodynamics, system properties, and process definitions.
  • Reversible and irreversible processes with engineering examples.
  • First law of thermodynamics for closed systems, internal energy, enthalpy, and specific heat.
  • Second law, heat engine, refrigerator and heat pump, and COP relations.
  • Kelvin-Planck and Clausius statements of the second law.
  • Modes of heat transfer: conduction, convection, and radiation.
  • Fourier law, one-dimensional steady conduction through plane wall, and Stefan-Boltzmann law.
  • Emissive power, emissivity, and concept of black body.

Important Topics

  • Damped vs forced oscillation equations and resonance behavior.
  • Compound pendulum and torsion pendulum applications.
  • Standing wave, resonance, and sonometer derivation patterns.
  • Reverberation, Sabine equation, and noise control methods.
  • Laser generation conditions and optical fiber acceptance angle.
  • Dielectric polarization and Clausius-Mossotti relation.
  • Maxwell equations and EM wave equation linkage.
  • de Broglie hypothesis, operators, and Schrodinger framework.
  • First and second law applications in closed systems.
  • Conduction/convection/radiation with Fourier and Stefan-Boltzmann laws.

Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate free, damped, and forced oscillation and solve one numerical on compound or torsion pendulum.
  2. Derive wave speed in a stretched string and solve one standing-wave or sonometer problem.
  3. Explain reverberation with Sabine equation and discuss acoustic treatment of a hall.
  4. Describe laser generation conditions and compute acceptance angle or numerical aperture for optical fiber.
  5. Solve a dielectric polarization or capacitor charging/discharging problem.
  6. Write Maxwell equations and derive the relation between E, B, and speed of light.
  7. Explain de Broglie hypothesis and write the time-dependent/time-independent Schrodinger equations.
  8. Apply first and second laws of thermodynamics to a closed system and classify heat transfer modes.
