Introduction

Instrumentation in Semester 2 develops the measurement and signal-handling foundation required in engineering systems. The subject focuses on how physical variables are sensed, conditioned, converted, analyzed, displayed, and recorded. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course helps students understand both the theory of measurement accuracy and the practical behavior of instruments used in laboratories and industrial environments.

The syllabus begins with instrumentation system elements, error/uncertainty analysis, bridge circuits, and transducer-based measurements. It then moves to analog instrument principles, multimeter ranges, power/energy meters, and instrument transformers. These units build confidence in selecting and interpreting measurement tools correctly.

Advanced units cover op-amp based signal conditioning, data acquisition architectures, ADC/DAC techniques, wave/spectrum analyzers, digital counters, oscilloscopes, and recording/display/storage devices. Together, these topics create a full measurement pipeline view that is useful in electronics, control, communication, and embedded engineering work.

What you will learn

  • Explain functional blocks of instrumentation and measurement systems.
  • Evaluate static errors, uncertainties, and calibration effects.
  • Apply bridge-based measurement methods and transducer concepts.
  • Use analog instrument principles for voltage, current, resistance, and power measurements.
  • Design basic signal conditioning chains with op-amp and filters.
  • Differentiate data acquisition architectures and transmission modes.
  • Compare ADC/DAC methods with quantization and performance considerations.
  • Interpret analyzer, oscilloscope, recorder, and display outputs correctly.

Syllabus Overview

Unit 1: Introduction to Instrumentation System (10 hrs)

  • Typical applications and functional elements: input (transducers/electrodes), intermediate (signal conditioning), and output (display/storage).
  • Errors, uncertainties, and static performance parameters: accuracy, precision, resolution, threshold, sensitivity, linearity, hysteresis, dead band, backlash, drift, and span.
  • Impedance loading/matching and standards of measurement.
  • Bridge measurements: Wheatstone (DC), Kelvin, Hay, Maxwell, Schering, Wien (AC), and Wagner ground connection.
  • Physical variables and transducer principles with resistive, capacitive, and inductive transducers.
  • Measurement of mechanical/process/biophysical variables and transducer calibration.
  • Measurement of voltage/current using moving-coil and moving-iron instruments; low/medium/high resistances.

Unit II: Principle of Analog Instruments (7 hrs)

  • Review of DC/AC voltmeter and ammeter using D'Arsonval principle.
  • DC multirange ammeter and voltmeter range extension methods.
  • AC voltmeter and multirange voltmeter.
  • Ohm meter and electronic multimeter operation.
  • Multimeter use as microammeter and DC ammeter.
  • Wattmeter, energy meter, and power factor meter principles.
  • Instrument transformer role and use.

Unit III: Electrical Signal Processing and Data Acquisition

  • Op-amp basics and instrumentation amplifier.
  • Signal amplification, attenuation, integration, differentiation, isolation, and wave shaping.
  • Noise effects and analog/digital filter context.
  • Data acquisition systems: analog, digital, single-channel, multi-channel, PC-based.
  • Series and parallel transmission with RS232 and IEEE-488/GPIB interface awareness.
  • Optical communication, fiber optics, and electro-optic conversion devices.

Unit IV: Data Converter and Connectors (8 hrs)

  • ADC and DAC principles with basic specifications.
  • Quantization error concept.
  • ADC types: flash, counter, successive approximation, dual-slope, delta-sigma introduction.
  • DAC types: weighted-resistor, R-2R ladder, and PWM DAC.
  • Probes and connectors: test leads, shielded cables, low-capacitance, high-voltage, and current probes.

Unit V: Wave Analyzers and Digital Instruments (8 hrs)

  • Frequency-selective and heterodyne wave analyzers.
  • Spectrum analyzer fundamentals with swept-receiver design.
  • Distortion analyzer (harmonic distortion, fundamental suppression type).
  • Frequency/time measurements: frequency counter, period counter, and counter errors.
  • Digital voltmeter types: ramp, integrating, servo-potentiometer, and successive approximation.
  • Vector voltmeter, digital multimeter, and computer-based digital instruments (IEEE-488 GPIB).

Unit VI: Recorders, Displays and Storage Devices (5 hrs)

  • Oscilloscope fundamentals: CRT, vertical/horizontal deflection, delay lines, probes, and transducers.
  • Oscilloscope specifications and measurement techniques.
  • Special oscilloscopes such as storage/sampling variants.
  • Recorders: basic recording systems, strip chart, galvanometer, and potentiometer type (direct/null).
  • Indicators and display devices: Nixie, LED, LCD, seven-segment, and dot-matrix.
  • Storage/output devices: magnetic tape/disc recorders, data loggers, dot-matrix and laser printers, compact/optical disc recorders.

Important Topics

  • Accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and error propagation in measurement.
  • Bridge circuit selection (Wheatstone, Kelvin, Hay, Maxwell, Schering, Wien) and balancing.
  • Transducer calibration and practical uncertainty handling.
  • Multirange meter extension calculations.
  • Instrumentation amplifier and signal conditioning chain design.
  • Single-channel vs multi-channel data acquisition architecture with interface choice.
  • ADC/DAC type comparison by speed, resolution, and use case.
  • Quantization and conversion error implications.
  • Wave analyzer vs spectrum analyzer application contexts.
  • DVM/DMM types and oscilloscope measurement interpretation.

Practice Questions

  1. Explain the functional blocks of an instrumentation system with a practical example.
  2. Differentiate accuracy and precision and solve a short uncertainty analysis problem.
  3. Derive range extension method for a multirange voltmeter or ammeter using D'Arsonval movement.
  4. Design a signal conditioning chain using op-amp, instrumentation amplifier, and filtering stages.
  5. Compare flash, counter, SAR, and dual-slope ADC in terms of speed, accuracy, and application.
  6. Explain quantization error and its effect on digitized measurement quality.
  7. Write short notes on frequency-selective wave analyzer, spectrum analyzer, and distortion analyzer.
  8. Describe oscilloscope measurement procedure for amplitude/time and name storage/display devices used in instrumentation.
