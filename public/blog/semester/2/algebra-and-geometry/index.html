Introduction

Algebra and Geometry in Semester 2 builds the mathematical structure required for later computational and engineering analysis courses. The subject combines linear algebra, vector spaces, optimization methods, infinite series, and analytic geometry in two and three dimensions. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course is not only a continuation of school mathematics; it is a transition to formal modeling and higher-dimensional reasoning.

The early units focus on matrices, linear systems, rank, transformations, eigen concepts, and vector spaces, which are foundational for graphics, machine learning, control systems, and many algorithmic methods. The Linear Programming unit adds optimization thinking and introduces simplex-oriented techniques that are directly useful in engineering decision problems.

The second half develops vector algebra and coordinate geometry in two and three dimensions. Topics such as conic sections, straight lines, planes, spheres, cones, and cylinders strengthen geometric interpretation and equation handling. If you study this subject with regular derivation practice and diagram-supported revision, it becomes one of the strongest scoring subjects in Semester 2.

What you will learn

  • Solve systems of linear equations using matrix methods and rank concepts.
  • Use vector space ideas including linear dependence and transformations.
  • Compute eigenvalues/eigenvectors and apply Cayley-Hamilton theorem basics.
  • Formulate and solve linear programming problems using simplex variants.
  • Apply vector algebra operations including scalar/vector products.
  • Test convergence behavior of sequences and infinite series.
  • Handle ellipse, hyperbola, and general conic equations in 2D geometry.
  • Model lines, planes, spheres, cones, and cylinders in 3D geometry.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Matrix and System of Linear Equations (4 hrs.)

  • System of linear equations, classification, and matrix/vector representation.
  • Solution by Cramer's rule and Gauss elimination method.
  • Rank of a matrix using elementary transformations and equivalent matrices.
  • Rank by echelon (triangular) and canonical (normal) forms.
  • Consistency of linear systems using coefficient and augmented matrix rank.

Unit II: Vector Space (7 hrs.)

  • Vectors and vector spaces, including R2 and R3 with subspaces and basis.
  • Linear dependence and independence with rank interpretation.
  • Linear transformation and transformation by matrix multiplication.
  • Orthogonal matrices and transformation by orthogonal matrix.
  • Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, characteristic equation, and properties of eigenvalues.
  • Cayley-Hamilton theorem (statement and verification only), including inverse up to 3 x 3 matrix.
  • Similar matrices and diagonalization (up to 2 x 2 matrices).

Unit III: Linear Programming Problems (5 hrs.)

  • Introduction to LPP, model formulation, and standard form.
  • Solution by simplex method.
  • Duality and dual simplex method.
  • Big-M method for mixed inequalities.

Unit IV: Vector Algebra (6 hrs.)

  • Review of vectors in coordinate form.
  • Scalar and vector product of two vectors.
  • Vector and scalar product of three and four vectors with physical interpretation.
  • Reciprocal system of vectors and related properties/problems.

Unit V: Infinite Series (5 hrs.)

  • Infinite sequence, infinite series, convergence, and invariance of convergence.
  • Necessary condition for convergence (divergence test) and infinite geometric series.
  • Series with positive terms: p-series, comparison test, ratio test, and root test.
  • Alternating series and Leibnitz test, absolute convergence.
  • Power series with interval and radius of convergence.

Unit VI: Two-dimensional Geometry (6 hrs.)

  • Coordinate transformation by shift of origin and rotation of axes.
  • Combined transformation and reduction of general equations to standard form.
  • Ellipse: standard equation, tangent, normal, and condition of tangency.
  • Hyperbola: standard/rectangular/conjugate forms with tangent and normal.
  • General equation of conic sections in Cartesian and polar forms.

Unit VII: Three-dimensional Geometry (12 hrs.)

  • Review of coordinates in space and equation of plane.
  • Straight line in symmetric and general forms, coplanarity, and shortest distance.
  • Sphere equations, diameter form, plane section, and tangent plane condition.
  • Cone equations with given vertex/generator and right circular cone problems.
  • Cylinder equations with given generator and right circular cylinder problems.

Important Topics

  • Cramer's rule and Gauss elimination for linear systems.
  • Rank-based consistency classification of linear equations.
  • Eigenvalue/eigenvector derivation steps.
  • Cayley-Hamilton verification and matrix inverse application.
  • Simplex tableau workflow and pivot logic.
  • Duality interpretation and Big-M setup.
  • Scalar, vector, and mixed product identities.
  • Convergence tests: comparison, ratio, root, and Leibnitz.
  • Conic section equation recognition and parameter extraction.
  • 3D line-plane equation conversions.
  • Sphere-cone-cylinder equation modeling.

Practice Questions

  1. Solve a system of three linear equations using Cramer's method and verify consistency by rank.
  2. Find eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a matrix and verify the Cayley-Hamilton theorem.
  3. Formulate an LPP from a word problem and solve it using simplex method.
  4. Apply Big-M method to a mixed-constraint optimization problem.
  5. Compute vector/scalar products and interpret geometric meaning of the result.
  6. Test convergence of one positive-term series and one alternating series using suitable tests.
  7. Derive and analyze an ellipse or hyperbola equation from given geometric conditions.
  8. Find equation of a sphere/cone/cylinder under specified constraints in 3D.
