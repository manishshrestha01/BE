Introduction

Calculus II in Semester 3 extends the mathematical foundation of Calculus I into methods that are directly useful in engineering modeling and analysis. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course combines multivariable integration, special-function based differential equation techniques, transform methods, and vector calculus theorems in one structured sequence. Instead of viewing topics as isolated formulas, students are expected to connect each method with physical interpretation such as area and volume computation, field analysis, and system response modeling.

The first half of the syllabus develops applied analytical tools. Multiple integrals in Cartesian and polar forms, Jacobian-based variable changes, and triple integrals in cylindrical or spherical coordinates train students to solve higher-dimensional geometry and mass-volume style problems. Series methods such as power series and Frobenius approaches then prepare learners to solve differential equations when elementary closed-form methods are not practical. Laplace transform is introduced as a systematic approach for solving initial value problems in ordinary differential equations with shifting theorems, convolution, and transform-domain operations.

The second half emphasizes Advanced Vector Calculus, Fourier Series, and introductory partial differential equations. Gradient, divergence, curl, line/surface integrals, and major integral theorems (Green, Stokes, Gauss) build conceptual depth for later electromagnetics, fluid-like models, and numerical methods. Fourier series develops periodic-function representation, while PDE topics introduce conservation-law style interpretation with engineering relevance. With regular derivation practice and tutorial-based revision, Calculus II becomes a high-value mathematical toolkit for later semesters.

What you will learn

  • Evaluate double and triple integrals using suitable coordinate systems.
  • Apply Jacobian transformation and change of integration order correctly.
  • Use power series and Frobenius methods for differential equation solutions.
  • Work with Legendre and Bessel equations at introductory problem level.
  • Use Laplace and inverse Laplace methods for IVP solving.
  • Interpret gradient, divergence, curl, and potential functions in vector fields.
  • Apply Green, Stokes, and Gauss theorems in integral evaluation tasks.
  • Represent periodic signals using Fourier series and connect PDE basics to engineering applications.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Multiple Integrals (6 Hours)

  • Introduction to multiple integrals and engineering context.
  • Double integrals in Cartesian and polar forms with Fubini theorem (statement only).
  • Change of order of integration and Jacobian-based variable transformation in double integrals.
  • Triple integrals in Cartesian form and Dirichlet integral ideas.
  • Use of cylindrical and spherical coordinates in triple integrals.
  • Applications to area and volume by double and triple integrals.

Unit II: Series Solution of Differential Equations and Special Functions (6 Hours)

  • Power series method for differential equation solving.
  • Legendre equation and Legendre polynomials of lower orders.
  • Graph interpretation of selected Legendre polynomials.
  • Frobenius method for series solution near singular points.
  • Bessel equation, Bessel functions, and basic properties.

Unit III: Laplace Transform and Its Application (8 Hours)

  • Laplace transform and inverse Laplace transform fundamentals.
  • Linearity, transforms of elementary functions, first shifting theorem.
  • Existence theorem (without proof) and uniqueness concept.
  • Transform of derivatives/integrals and differentiation/integration in transform domain.
  • Unit step and periodic functions in LT with second shifting theorem.
  • Convolution theorem and inverse transform applications.
  • Application of Laplace transform to solve ordinary differential equation IVPs.

Unit IV: Advanced Vector Calculus (15 Hours)

  • Differentiation of vector function of scalar variable.
  • Point functions, gradient, directional derivative, divergence, and curl with properties.
  • Line integral with physical interpretation and path evaluation.
  • Potential function and path independence of line integrals.
  • Green theorem in plane (without proof) and applications.
  • Surface integrals and evaluation methods.
  • Stokes theorem (without proof) and applications.
  • Gauss divergence theorem (without proof) and applications.

Unit V: Fourier Series (5 Hours)

  • Periodic, odd, and even function review.
  • Fourier series representation of periodic functions in standard intervals.
  • Sine and cosine series for odd/even function decomposition.

Unit VII: Partial Differential Equations (5 Hours)

  • Introduction to partial differential equations.
  • Linear constant coefficient equations.
  • Applications in conservation laws, breaking time, shock waves, nonlinear advection, and traffic flow.

Important Topics

  • Order-change and Jacobian conversion in double integrals.
  • Cylindrical and spherical setup for triple integral limits.
  • Power series and Frobenius workflow for DEs.
  • Legendre and Bessel equation standard forms.
  • Laplace shifting theorems and convolution applications.
  • Gradient, divergence, curl, and physical interpretation.
  • Path independence and conservative field checks.
  • Green, Stokes, and Gauss theorem application patterns.
  • Fourier coefficient derivation for odd/even functions.
  • First-order PDE modeling intuition in conservation-type problems.

Practice Questions

  1. Evaluate a double integral by changing order and converting to polar form where appropriate.
  2. Set up and compute a triple integral in cylindrical or spherical coordinates for volume.
  3. Solve a differential equation using power series or Frobenius method.
  4. Find Laplace and inverse Laplace transforms using shifting and convolution rules.
  5. Use Laplace transform to solve an initial value ordinary differential equation.
  6. Compute line integral and verify whether the field is conservative.
  7. Apply Green or Stokes or Gauss theorem to simplify an integral calculation.
  8. Expand a periodic function into Fourier series and identify sine/cosine components.
