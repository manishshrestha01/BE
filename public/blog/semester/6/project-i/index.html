Introduction

Project I in Semester 6 is a structured team-based practical course where students design and build a real software, hardware, or embedded solution to address a practical problem. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course is intended to shift students from classroom-only learning to implementation discipline. Teams are expected to apply earlier-semester knowledge in requirements analysis, architecture, development, testing, and communication while using tools relevant to current industry practice.

The course runs across the entire semester and is assessed continuously through three formal phases: proposal, mid-term, and final defense. This phased format teaches project lifecycle execution rather than last-week completion. Students must demonstrate planning quality, technical progress, documentation consistency, and teamwork at each checkpoint. The expected progression is clear: define a feasible problem and scope, implement with supervision and validation, then deliver a complete demonstrable output.

Project I also prepares the foundation for Project II when teams choose to continue in the same thematic direction. Strong teams treat this course as product development with milestone tracking, risk control, and regular supervisor review. If you maintain disciplined documentation, evidence-based decisions, and iterative testing from the beginning, Project I becomes one of the most valuable courses for internship interviews and professional readiness.

What you will learn

  • Form project teams and define feasible real-world problem statements.
  • Prepare proposal documents including objective, scope, requirements, and timeline.
  • Apply suitable tools, platforms, and engineering practices during development.
  • Build architecture/design and validate progress through milestone reviews.
  • Implement and test project components to produce a demonstrable output.
  • Prepare professional project reports and technical documentation.
  • Present and defend project decisions in formal mid-term and final viva settings.
  • Collaborate effectively using team roles, supervisor feedback, and iteration cycles.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Project Orientation and Team Workflow

  • Team formation (typically 3-4 members) and topic ideation.
  • Selection of practical, industry-relevant software/hardware/embedded project scope.
  • Assignment of supervisor and project governance process.
  • Semester-long execution planning with phased milestones.

Unit II: Proposal Phase (30% weight)

  • Feasibility study, title finalization, abstract, and objectives.
  • Requirements analysis and project specification.
  • Initial project plan with timeline and cost estimation.
  • Innovation/creativity and team contribution demonstration.
  • Proposal report preparation and formal proposal defense.

Unit III: Mid-term Phase (30% weight)

  • Incorporation of proposal-review feedback.
  • System/architectural design completion and validation strategy.
  • Evidence of substantial implementation progress (around 60% or more).
  • Progress report organization, consistency, and data/result analysis.
  • Formal mid-term defense with supervisor approval.

Unit IV: Final Phase and Defense (40% weight)

  • Completion of full lifecycle: analysis, design, coding, and testing.
  • Demonstrable final product output and technical presentation.
  • Final defense with internal/external examiners (including industry expert participation).
  • Final report submission as per university/college project guideline.
  • Evaluation on presentation performance, contribution, completeness, and viva quality.

Important Topics

  • Problem feasibility and scope control.
  • Requirement quality and architecture clarity.
  • Timeline planning and milestone discipline.
  • Supervisor-feedback incorporation.
  • Testing and validation evidence.
  • Documentation structure and consistency.
  • Team contribution traceability.
  • Presentation and viva preparedness.
  • Industry relevance and practical usability.
  • Extension planning toward Project II.

Practice Questions

  1. Draft a project problem statement, objective set, and feasibility summary suitable for Proposal Phase.
  2. Prepare a requirement specification and identify at least five measurable acceptance criteria.
  3. Design a semester timeline with milestones, risk register, and mitigation strategy.
  4. Create a system architecture diagram and justify major technology choices.
  5. Define validation and testing strategy for your project with expected metrics.
  6. Prepare a mid-term presentation checklist showing progress evidence and feedback incorporation.
  7. Outline the final report structure according to Project Guidelines and assign writing ownership per team member.
  8. Simulate a final viva by answering technical, managerial, and design-tradeoff questions for your project.
