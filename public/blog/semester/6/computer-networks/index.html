Introduction

Computer Networks in Semester 6 provides the layered systems view needed to understand how modern digital communication works from physical media to application protocols. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, the course is structured around architecture, addressing, routing, transport behavior, and operational security. Students are expected to connect theory with practical network setup, measurement, and troubleshooting skills.

The course starts with network taxonomy, topologies, protocol models, and network hardware, then builds through physical, data-link, and network-layer mechanisms. Topics such as framing, flow/error control, channel access, subnetting, routing algorithms, and IPv4/IPv6 comparisons create strong foundations for both exam problems and field implementation. Transport and congestion-control units further clarify how reliability, throughput, and quality of service are maintained under real traffic conditions.

The final units on application services and security emphasize deployable knowledge such as DNS, HTTP/FTP, email protocols, VPN, cryptography basics, firewalling, and IP security. Combined with practical tasks like packet analysis and router configuration, this course prepares students for infrastructure roles and advanced network/security study. Consistent protocol-flow visualization and subnetting practice are key to high performance in this subject.

What you will learn

  • Describe network models, architectures, and topology choices for communication systems.
  • Differentiate OSI and TCP/IP layers with related hardware and services.
  • Explain media, switching, and physical-layer performance parameters.
  • Apply data-link concepts including framing, error control, and channel access methods.
  • Perform IPv4 subnetting/CIDR planning and compare IPv4 with IPv6.
  • Interpret routing strategies and common protocols such as RIP, OSPF, and BGP.
  • Analyze TCP/UDP transport behavior, multiplexing, and socket-level communication basics.
  • Understand congestion control, QoS concepts, application-layer services, and core network-security mechanisms.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction to Computer Network (3 hrs)

  • Network definition, merits, and demerits.
  • Network models: PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN, and GAN.
  • Topological models: star, bus, distributed bus, mesh, tree, hybrid, and ring.
  • Client-server and peer-to-peer models.
  • Overview of ISPs, NSPs, and networking backbone.
  • Recent telecom trends: 2G/3G/4G/5G.

Unit II: Reference Model (4 hrs)

  • Protocols and standards.
  • Interfaces and services.
  • OSI layer model.
  • TCP/IP layered model.
  • Comparison of OSI and TCP/IP.
  • Networking hardware: NIC, hub, repeater, switch, bridge, router, gateway.

Unit III: Physical Layer (4 hrs)

  • Guided media: copper and fiber cabling standards.
  • Unguided media: Bluetooth, Wi-Fi/WLAN, microwave/radio-wave and satellite basics.
  • Circuit, packet, and message switching.
  • ISDN signaling and architecture.
  • Performance metrics: bandwidth, throughput, latency, bandwidth-delay product, and jitter.

Unit IV: Data Link Layer (8 hrs)

  • LLC and MAC sub-layer overview.
  • Physical (MAC) addressing.
  • Framing and data-link responsibilities.
  • Flow control: stop-and-wait, go-back-N, and selective-repeat.
  • Error detection (parity, CRC) and correction (Hamming code).
  • Channel access methods: ALOHA, CSMA, and CSMA/CD.
  • 802.3 Ethernet variants and token-based networks (802.4/802.5).
  • Virtual circuit switching: frame relay, ATM, and X.25.

Unit V: Network Layer Protocols and Addressing (8 hrs)

  • Logical addressing fundamentals.
  • IPv4 addressing, subnetting, supernetting, CIDR, and VLSM.
  • IPv6 addressing overview and IPv4/IPv6 header comparison.
  • Classful/classless routing and adaptive/non-adaptive routing.
  • Distance-vector and link-state routing.
  • Interior and exterior routing; unicast and multicast routing.
  • Routing protocols: RIP, OSPF, and BGP.
  • Network address translation (NAT).

Unit VI: Transport Layer and Protocols (4 hrs)

  • Port addressing and process-to-process delivery.
  • Multiplexing and demultiplexing.
  • TCP services, features, headers, ports, and handshaking.
  • UDP services, features, headers, and ports.
  • Socket programming concept for TCP and UDP.

Unit VII: Congestion Control and Quality of Services (3 hrs)

  • Congestion control: open-loop and closed-loop mechanisms.
  • Traffic shaping: leaky bucket and token bucket.
  • TCP congestion-control overview.

Unit VIII: Application Layer, Servers and Protocols (4 hrs)

  • Domain addressing, DNS server, and DNS queries.
  • HTTP, FTP, and proxy server overview.
  • DHCP principles.
  • Email protocols: SMTP, POP, and IMAP.

Unit IX: Network Management and Security (7 hrs)

  • Introduction to network management.
  • Cryptography basics: DES and RSA.
  • Key-exchange protocols: Diffie-Hellman and Kerberos.
  • VPN concepts.
  • Overview of IP security.
  • Firewall and digital certificate fundamentals.
  • Next generation network (NGN) overview.

Important Topics

  • OSI vs TCP/IP model mapping.
  • Subnetting, CIDR, and VLSM design logic.
  • Flow/error control at data-link layer.
  • Routing algorithm and protocol comparison.
  • TCP vs UDP behavior and use-cases.
  • Congestion and traffic-shaping mechanisms.
  • DNS, DHCP, and email protocol workflows.
  • Cryptography and key-exchange fundamentals.
  • Firewall and IP security basics.
  • Network-performance metrics interpretation.

Practice Questions

  1. Compare OSI and TCP/IP models and map key protocols to their corresponding layers.
  2. Design a subnetting scheme for a given organization using CIDR and VLSM constraints.
  3. Solve a framing and error-control scenario using CRC and Hamming-code reasoning.
  4. Analyze routing paths using distance-vector and link-state approaches and discuss protocol suitability.
  5. Differentiate TCP and UDP through a service case and explain socket-level communication setup.
  6. Apply leaky-bucket and token-bucket traffic-shaping concepts on a sample traffic stream.
  7. Trace a DNS request flow and explain how DHCP and email protocols integrate in enterprise networks.
  8. Outline a basic secure-network design using VPN, firewalls, and key-exchange principles.
