Introduction

Project II is the capstone practical course in Semester 8 where students must deliver a complete and demonstrable engineering solution. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course expects teams to plan, design, implement, test, and present a real-world software, hardware, or embedded product. It is the final stage where academic knowledge is translated into industry-level execution quality.

The course may be an extension of Project I, but it should establish clear advancement through additional intelligence, subsystem integration, or expanded scope. Students are expected to operate in small teams under supervision, follow structured milestones, and maintain professional documentation throughout the semester. The process is continuous, with formal checkpoints for proposal, mid-term, and final defense.

Project II evaluation emphasizes both technical depth and professional delivery: feasibility, architecture, implementation progress, validation, teamwork, report quality, demonstration, and viva performance. Students who manage timeline discipline, evidence-backed reporting, and iterative testing from the beginning generally perform better in both final defense and career opportunities after graduation.

What you will learn

  • Plan and execute a full-semester capstone project with clear milestones.
  • Extend or redesign a project into a complete production-ready solution.
  • Apply requirement analysis, architecture design, implementation, and validation workflows.
  • Work effectively as a team under supervisor and panel review.
  • Prepare proposal, progress, and final reports in prescribed format.
  • Present technical work with clear problem framing, outcomes, and demonstration quality.
  • Incorporate panel feedback between proposal, mid-term, and final phases.
  • Defend technical and managerial decisions confidently in viva.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Project Orientation and Working Procedure

  • Team formation (typically 3-4 members) and project-scope definition.
  • Selection of practical project relevant to local industry context.
  • Project execution under assigned faculty supervision.
  • Use of suitable platforms/tools for software or hardware development.
  • Requirement that Project II should maintain distinct technical entity and completion quality.

Unit II: Proposal Phase (30%)

  • Prepare conceptual and implementation framework and submit proposal report.
  • Formal proposal presentation/defense before panel.
  • Supervisor assignment and review approval process.
  • Evaluation focus: feasibility, objective, requirement specification, project plan, creativity, teamwork, and proposal documentation quality.

Unit III: Mid-term Phase (30%)

  • Present substantial project progress (typically around 60% or more).
  • Complete architectural design and validation scheme by mid-term.
  • Show implementational evidence and preliminary results.
  • Evaluation focus: proposal-feedback incorporation, system design quality, depth of progress, validation criteria, team effort, and mid-term documentation consistency.

Unit IV: Final Phase and Defense (40%)

  • Complete all phases including analysis, design, coding, and testing.
  • Demonstrate visible final output/product in formal final defense.
  • Appear before panel (including industry expert participation where applicable).
  • Evaluation focus: contribution, completeness, tools/techniques, presentation performance, project demonstration, viva, and final report quality.

Unit V: Final Submission and Guideline Compliance

  • Prepare hard-cover bound final report (black book) as required by department/university guideline.
  • Submit required hard copies to designated offices/records.
  • Submit soft copy in prescribed format (for example PDF or DOCX).
  • Follow official university/college Project Guidelines for layout and process compliance.

Important Topics

  • Capstone scope planning and feasibility control.
  • Proposal-to-implementation continuity.
  • Architecture and validation strategy quality.
  • Milestone discipline across three defense phases.
  • Evidence-backed progress reporting.
  • Panel-feedback incorporation quality.
  • Demonstration readiness and viva depth.
  • Final report and submission compliance.

Practice Questions

  1. Prepare a proposal abstract, objective set, and requirement summary for your Project II topic.
  2. Create a milestone timeline that maps proposal, mid-term, and final deliverables.
  3. Explain how your Project II extends or differentiates from Project I (if related).
  4. Design a validation plan with measurable performance or quality criteria.
  5. Draft a mid-term defense outline showing completed modules and pending scope.
  6. Prepare a final demonstration checklist covering functionality, stability, and user scenario flow.
  7. List expected viva questions on architecture, technology choice, and risk handling, with concise answers.
  8. Prepare a final-report compliance checklist based on your department’s Project Guideline.
