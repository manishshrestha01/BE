Introduction

Artificial Intelligence in Semester 5 provides a broad foundation for building systems that can perceive, reason, learn, and act. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course is designed to combine core AI problem-solving methods with practical approaches used in machine learning, knowledge-based systems, and uncertainty handling. It also includes ethical and societal dimensions, making students think beyond purely technical implementation.

The syllabus begins with AI fundamentals and intelligent-agent models, then advances to search-based problem solving. Students study uninformed and informed search, optimization heuristics, adversarial game search, and constraint-satisfaction frameworks. These topics establish the algorithmic backbone required for intelligent system design.

The later units focus on knowledge representation, probabilistic reasoning, machine learning, neural networks, fuzzy logic, and expert-system architecture. Together, they provide both symbolic and data-driven perspectives of intelligence. With regular implementation of algorithms and domain-based modeling exercises, students can build strong conceptual and practical competence in AI.

What you will learn

  • Explain AI foundations, approaches, and societal implications.
  • Model intelligent agents with rational behavior and task environments.
  • Formulate search problems and apply uninformed/informed search algorithms.
  • Use adversarial and CSP techniques for complex decision problems.
  • Represent knowledge with logic-based and semantic models.
  • Apply probabilistic reasoning using Bayesian and temporal models.
  • Use core supervised/unsupervised learning methods and ANN basics.
  • Design simple fuzzy and expert-system solutions for real-world problems.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (4 hrs)

  • Intelligence concepts, types, and components.
  • Artificial intelligence and major AI approaches.
  • Acting/thinking humanly vs acting/thinking rationally.
  • Foundations and history of AI.
  • Risk-benefit analysis, ethics, and societal implications.
  • AI and society: work, automation, employment, privacy, and security.
  • Governance and regulation context.

Unit II: Intelligent Agents (5 hrs)

  • Agents and environments.
  • Rationality, performance measures, and rational-agent concept.
  • Task environment and properties.
  • Agent structure and agent programs.
  • Types of agent programs and learning agents.

Unit III: Problem Solving and Search Algorithms (10 hrs)

  • Problem solving concepts, agents, and processes.
  • Production systems and problem formulation.
  • Uninformed search: BFS, DFS, iterative deepening.
  • Informed search and heuristics: greedy best-first, A*.
  • Local search and optimization: hill climbing, simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, gradient descent.
  • Adversarial search: minimax and alpha-beta pruning.
  • Constraint satisfaction problems: variables, domains, constraints.
  • CSP search: backtracking, constraint propagation, and min-conflicts heuristic.

Unit IV: Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (10 hrs)

  • Propositional logic: syntax, semantics, inference, CNF, and resolution.
  • Limitations of propositional logic.
  • Predicate logic: syntax, semantics, inference, and resolution.
  • Reasoning under uncertainty and probabilistic reasoning.
  • Bayesian networks and probabilistic reasoning over time.
  • Hidden Markov models.
  • Other representation approaches: semantic nets, rule-based, and ontological representation.

Unit V: Machine Learning (6 hrs)

  • Definition and evolution of machine learning.
  • Learning by analogy and explanation-based learning.
  • Supervised learning: classification and regression.
  • Linear regression and k-nearest neighbor.
  • Unsupervised learning: clustering and k-means.
  • Artificial neural networks: inspiration, components, and training.
  • Forward propagation, loss function, backward propagation, learning rate.
  • Single-layer and multi-layer perceptron.

Unit VI: Fuzzy Logic (5 hrs)

  • Classical vs fuzzy logic.
  • Fuzzy sets and membership functions.
  • Fuzzy operations and rule-based systems.
  • Fuzzification and defuzzification.
  • Mamdani fuzzy inference system.

Unit VII: Expert System (5 hrs)

  • Definition, history, and architecture of expert systems.
  • Knowledge representation: logic-based, rule-based, semantic networks, ontology-based, frame-based.
  • Inference mechanisms: forward and backward chaining.
  • Knowledge acquisition, learning, and application domains.

Important Topics

  • AI approach comparison and rational-agent formulation.
  • Search strategy trade-offs between BFS, DFS, and A*.
  • Heuristic design for optimization quality.
  • CSP modeling and constraint-propagation efficiency.
  • Resolution-based reasoning in propositional and predicate logic.
  • Bayesian and temporal probabilistic reasoning.
  • Supervised vs unsupervised learning use-case fit.
  • ANN training pipeline and learning-rate effects.
  • Fuzzy inference and defuzzification methods.
  • Expert-system knowledge representation and chaining mechanisms.

Practice Questions

  1. Compare AI approaches and classify a system as acting/thinking humanly/rationally.
  2. Model a task environment and design an appropriate intelligent-agent type.
  3. Solve a search problem using BFS, DFS, and A* and compare outcomes.
  4. Formulate a CSP and solve it using backtracking with constraint propagation.
  5. Convert a logic statement to CNF and apply resolution to infer conclusions.
  6. Build and interpret a simple Bayesian network for uncertain reasoning.
  7. Implement one supervised and one unsupervised learning algorithm on sample data.
  8. Design a basic fuzzy or rule-based expert system for a real-world decision task.
