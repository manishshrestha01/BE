Introduction

Digital Signal Analysis and Processing in Semester 5 builds the theoretical and computational foundation of modern DSP systems. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course explains how discrete-time signals are analyzed, transformed, filtered, and implemented in practical digital processing pipelines. It links mathematical transform methods with filter-design techniques and algorithmic implementation.

The course starts with discrete signals, system properties, convolution, and Fourier-domain analysis. It then introduces Z-transform as a central tool for system characterization, causality/stability interpretation, and inverse analysis. Frequency-domain understanding is expanded through pole-zero relationships and system-response behavior.

The later units focus on FIR/IIR structures, digital filter design methods, and DFT/FFT computation. These topics are essential for real-world tasks such as noise reduction, spectral estimation, and communication-signal analysis. With consistent numerical practice and simulation-based labs, students can develop strong DSP design intuition and implementation skill.

What you will learn

  • Classify and analyze discrete-time signals and systems.
  • Apply DTFS/DTFT concepts and convolution for LTI analysis.
  • Use Z-transform and ROC properties for system interpretation.
  • Relate pole-zero patterns to causality, stability, and frequency response.
  • Represent FIR/IIR filters in common structures.
  • Design IIR filters using impulse invariance and bilinear transform methods.
  • Design FIR filters using windowing and frequency-sampling approaches.
  • Compute DFT/FFT and evaluate algorithmic complexity.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Discrete Signals and Systems (8 hrs)

  • Discrete-time signals and basic signal types.
  • Energy/power signals and periodicity concepts.
  • Independent-variable transformations.
  • Discrete-time Fourier series and DTFT properties.
  • Discrete-time system properties.
  • LTI systems, convolution sum, and LTI properties.
  • Frequency response of LTI systems.
  • Sampling of continuous-time signals and spectral properties.

Unit II: Z-Transform (6 hrs)

  • Definition, convergence, and region of convergence (ROC).
  • Z-transform properties: linearity, time shift, exponential scaling, differentiation, reversal, convolution, multiplication.
  • Inverse Z-transform by long division and partial fraction methods.
  • Causality, non-causality, and stability conditions via ROC.

Unit III: Analysis of LTI System in Frequency Domain (5 hrs)

  • Magnitude/phase response interpretation of LTI systems.
  • Response to complex exponential signals.
  • Linear constant-coefficient difference equations and system functions.
  • Pole-zero relationships with frequency response.
  • Linear phase behavior and relation to causality.

Unit IV: Discrete Filter Structures (6 hrs)

  • FIR filter structures: direct, cascade, lattice.
  • IIR filter structures: direct I/II, cascade, lattice, lattice-ladder.
  • Quantization effects: truncation, rounding, limit cycles, scaling.

Unit V: IIR Filter Design (7 hrs)

  • IIR design by impulse invariance method.
  • IIR design by bilinear transformation.
  • Digital low-pass Butterworth filter design.
  • Properties of Chebyshev, elliptic, and Bessel filters.
  • Spectral transformations: LP prototype to LP/HP/BP/BS.

Unit VI: FIR Filter Design (7 hrs)

  • Gibbs phenomenon.
  • Window-based FIR design with rectangular, Hanning, and Hamming windows.
  • Kaiser-window design approach.
  • Frequency-sampling method.
  • Optimum approximation and Remez exchange algorithm.

Unit VII: Discrete Fourier Transform (6 hrs)

  • DFT representation and core properties (linearity, shifting, duality, symmetry, convolution, multiplication).
  • Circular convolution concepts.
  • FFT algorithms: decimation-in-time and decimation-in-frequency.
  • Computational complexity of FFT.

Important Topics

  • Convolution and LTI response interpretation.
  • ROC-based system classification and stability checks.
  • Pole-zero frequency-response linkage.
  • Difference-equation based system analysis.
  • FIR/IIR structural realization trade-offs.
  • Quantization impact on digital filters.
  • Bilinear transform vs impulse invariance design behavior.
  • Window choice effects in FIR design.
  • Circular convolution and DFT property usage.
  • FFT complexity advantage over direct DFT.

Practice Questions

  1. Classify a discrete signal and compute convolution output for an LTI system.
  2. Find Z-transform and inverse Z-transform of sequences and identify ROC.
  3. Determine system stability and causality using pole/ROC information.
  4. Realize a given transfer function in direct and cascade forms.
  5. Design a low-pass IIR filter using bilinear or impulse invariance method.
  6. Design an FIR filter using window method and compare response.
  7. Compute DFT and circular convolution for a finite sequence.
  8. Implement FFT (DIT or DIF) for 8-point sequence and compare operations with DFT.
