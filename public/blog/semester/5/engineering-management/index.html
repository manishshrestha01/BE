Introduction

Engineering Management in Semester 5 introduces management principles tailored to technology-driven organizations. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this course is designed to build practical leadership and organizational skills needed by engineers who will work on projects, teams, and operational systems. It bridges management theory with ICT-specific workplace challenges.

The syllabus begins with management foundations, organizational structures, and engineering-manager roles. It then moves to planning and organizing practices across strategic, tactical, and operational levels. These units provide a framework for resource alignment and structured execution in engineering projects.

Later units focus on motivation, leadership, human-resource management, and control mechanisms, followed by emerging management trends. This sequence helps students understand both people-centric and system-centric aspects of project delivery. With case-based discussion and structured reflection, students gain practical readiness for technical leadership roles.

What you will learn

  • Explain management principles and engineering-manager responsibilities.
  • Apply planning methods at strategic, tactical, and operational levels.
  • Analyze organization structures for ICT enterprises.
  • Use motivation and leadership concepts for technical teams.
  • Understand HR activities including recruitment, training, and appraisal.
  • Apply control processes and ICT-enabled monitoring techniques.
  • Address workforce challenges in technology organizations.
  • Interpret emerging trends in engineering management for ICT projects.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction (6 hrs)

  • Management concepts, functions, scope, and principles.
  • Organization characteristics and types (formal, informal, virtual).
  • Engineering management in technology-driven environments.
  • Engineering functions in organizations: product development, operations, IT systems, and quality assurance.
  • Roles and responsibilities of an engineering manager.

Unit II: Planning and Organizing (6 hrs)

  • Planning levels: strategic, tactical, operational.
  • Steps, tools, and importance of planning.
  • Organizing process and organization structure.
  • Types of structure: traditional (line, functional) and modern (matrix, network, hybrid).
  • Emerging planning and organizing issues for ICT enterprises.

Unit III: Motivation and Leadership (6 hrs)

  • Motivation theories: Maslow, Herzberg, expectancy, and equity.
  • Motivation techniques for technical workforce.
  • Leadership styles: autocratic, democratic, servant, transformational.
  • Characteristics of learning organizations in ICT.
  • Challenges and strategies for motivating and leading technical teams.

Unit IV: Human Resource Management and Control (8 hrs)

  • Functions of human resource management.
  • Job analysis, specification, and description.
  • Recruitment, selection, and training (on/off-job).
  • Performance appraisal methods.
  • Challenges in managing ICT workforce.
  • Control: importance, process, types, and techniques.
  • ICT tools for effective control of engineering projects and organizations.

Unit V: Emerging Trends in Engineering Management (4 hrs)

  • Participative management, conflict resolution, and change management.
  • Quality management, innovation management, and disruption.
  • Recent management concepts for ICT-based projects and organizations.

Important Topics

  • Management function integration for engineering projects.
  • Structure selection for scale and agility.
  • Planning-tool usage and execution alignment.
  • Leadership style selection by team maturity.
  • Motivation strategy for technical workforce retention.
  • HR lifecycle management in ICT environments.
  • Performance appraisal and feedback loop design.
  • Control mechanisms with ICT dashboards/tools.
  • Conflict/change management in fast-evolving teams.
  • Innovation-oriented management for digital products.

Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate management functions and apply them to a software project.
  2. Design a planning framework with strategic, tactical, and operational components.
  3. Compare organizational structures and justify one for a growing IT company.
  4. Apply one motivation theory and one leadership style to a technical-team case.
  5. Prepare a basic HR plan including recruitment, training, and appraisal steps.
  6. Design a control mechanism using ICT tools for project progress tracking.
  7. Analyze a change-management or conflict-resolution scenario in an engineering team.
  8. Propose an innovation-management strategy for an ICT product organization.
