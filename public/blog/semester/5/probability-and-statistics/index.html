Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering semester 5 tutorial for Probability and Statistics (course code MTH 216) with syllabus, key topics, concepts, and 5 practice questions.

Introduction

Probability and Statistics in Semester 5 provides the analytical foundation needed for data-driven engineering decisions. In Pokhara University BE Computer Engineering, this subject is structured to connect descriptive analysis, probabilistic reasoning, inference, and predictive modeling in one practical flow. It helps students move from raw data representation to formal conclusions and engineering interpretation.

The course begins with descriptive statistics and probability laws, then develops both discrete and continuous distributions that are frequently used in reliability analysis, risk modeling, and computational estimation. The transition to bivariate random variables and joint distributions expands this single-variable viewpoint into multi-variable relationships. This is a key step for understanding dependencies in real-world datasets.

The final units on sampling, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, and regression equip students with core statistical inference skills used in quality assessment, experimentation, and performance studies. Students who practice both conceptual derivations and numerical problem solving regularly usually gain strong confidence in statistical decision-making and score well in exams.

What you will learn

  • Summarize and present engineering data using descriptive statistical measures.
  • Apply probability laws and Bayes theorem to uncertain events.
  • Work with discrete and continuous random variables and expectations.
  • Use standard discrete distributions like binomial and Poisson models.
  • Apply continuous distributions such as normal, gamma, beta, and exponential.
  • Interpret joint and marginal probability functions for bivariate variables.
  • Construct confidence intervals and estimate population parameters.
  • Perform hypothesis tests, ANOVA, chi-square, and regression analysis.

Syllabus Overview

Unit I: Introduction and Descriptive Statistics (4 hrs)

  • Introduction to statistics and applications in engineering.
  • Collection and presentation of data (diagrammatic and graphical methods).
  • Measures of central tendency, location, and variability.

Unit II: Probability (8 hrs)

  • Basic probability and foundational laws.
  • Additive law, multiplicative law, and Bayes theorem.
  • Random variables (discrete and continuous) and probability distribution function.
  • Mathematical expectation of random variables.

Unit III: Discrete Probability Distributions (4 hrs)

  • Binomial distribution.
  • Poisson distribution.
  • Negative binomial distribution.
  • Hypergeometric distribution.

Unit IV: Continuous Probability Distributions (6 hrs)

  • Rectangular (uniform) distribution.
  • Normal distribution.
  • Gamma and beta distributions.
  • Exponential distribution.

Unit V: Bivariate Random Variables and Joint Probability Distribution (4 hrs)

  • Joint probability mass function and marginal probability mass function.
  • Joint probability density function and marginal probability density function.

Unit VI: Sampling Distribution and Estimation (7 hrs)

  • Review of terms used in sampling.
  • Probability and non-probability sampling.
  • Sampling distribution of mean and standard error.
  • Central limit theorem.
  • Point and interval estimation concepts.
  • Sample size determination.
  • Confidence intervals for single mean, difference of means, and population proportion.

Unit VII: Hypothesis Testing (8 hrs)

  • Basic concepts in hypothesis testing.
  • One-sample test for mean and proportion.
  • Two-sample test for means and proportions.
  • Paired t-test.
  • ANOVA.
  • Chi-square test of independence.

Unit VIII: Correlation and Regression (7 hrs)

  • Simple correlation and its properties.
  • Simple linear regression.
  • Multiple regression (with two independent variables).

Important Topics

  • Data summarization and variability interpretation.
  • Bayes theorem and conditional probability reasoning.
  • Expectation and variance of random variables.
  • Distribution selection for engineering event models.
  • Normal distribution and standardization workflows.
  • Joint, marginal, and conditional distribution relationships.
  • Confidence interval construction and sample-size logic.
  • Hypothesis testing steps and decision errors.
  • ANOVA and chi-square applicability conditions.
  • Regression interpretation and model assumption checks.

Practice Questions

  1. Summarize a dataset using graphical presentation and central tendency/variability measures.
  2. Apply Bayes theorem to update probabilities in an engineering scenario.
  3. Compute expectation and variance for a given discrete random variable.
  4. Solve numerical problems using binomial, Poisson, and exponential distributions.
  5. Find joint and marginal probabilities for a bivariate random variable table.
  6. Construct confidence intervals for mean and proportion from sample data.
  7. Perform one-sample and two-sample hypothesis tests and interpret decisions.
  8. Fit and interpret a simple or multiple linear regression model.
